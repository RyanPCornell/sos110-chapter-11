Approximate central thresholds from Armstrong McKay et al. (2022). Crossing a threshold does not mean instant collapse — it marks where a self-reinforcing change becomes likely, sometimes over centuries.
⚠️ Educational estimates only. This is a simplified climate emulator, not a full Earth-system model. It uses the IPCC's near-linear relationship between cumulative CO₂ emissions and warming (TCRE) plus a fixed non-CO₂ forcing offset. Real projections from models like those in the IPCC AR6 include ocean heat lag, aerosols, methane, and many feedbacks this tool approximates. Numbers are for building intuition, not for planning or research.
Sources & methodology
[1] IPCC. (2021). Climate Change 2021: The Physical Science Basis (AR6, WGI), Summary for Policymakers & Ch. 5. Transient Climate Response to Cumulative Emissions (TCRE) ≈ 0.45 °C per 1,000 Gt CO₂ (likely range 0.27–0.63); ~1.1 °C warming by 2011–2020; SSP scenario warming ranges used to calibrate the presets.
[2] Matthews, H.D., et al. (2009). The proportionality of global warming to cumulative carbon emissions. Nature, 459, 829–832. Foundational TCRE relationship.
[3] Meinshausen, M., et al. (2020). The SSP greenhouse gas concentrations and their extensions to 2500. Geoscientific Model Development, 13, 3571–3605. Scenario emission pathways.
[4] Friedlingstein, P., et al. (2023). Global Carbon Budget 2023. Earth System Science Data. ~40 Gt CO₂/yr present-day emissions; airborne fraction ~0.46; 7.8 Gt CO₂ per ppm.
[5] Armstrong McKay, D.I., et al. (2022). Exceeding 1.5 °C global warming could trigger multiple climate tipping points. Science, 377, eabn7950. Tipping-point threshold estimates.
